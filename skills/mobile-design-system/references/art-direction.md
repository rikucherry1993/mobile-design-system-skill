# Art direction: from a visual proposition to a system

Read this before proposing theme directions. Tokens are implementation decisions, not the starting
concept.

## Why a coherent design succeeds

A mature result is a small number of mutually reinforcing decisions. A clear premise limits what
belongs; composition gives the eye a route; typography makes the content credible; materials and
images support that premise. Detail is consistent with the whole.

Transfer the method, not a successful project's aesthetic. A handmade monochrome workspace can
succeed through sparse marks, irregular strokes and theatrical type. A shopping app may need
credible photography, calm navigation and precise price typography instead. The common method is
thesis → representative screens → calibration with the user → reusable rules (tokens).

## Write the proposition before tokens

For each direction, write one sentence: "For [actual task/audience], [specific visual treatment]
makes [benefit] visible." Then connect the decisions:

- **Hierarchy:** what is noticed first, second and third? Where does the eye rest? Which action is
  primary? Name the geometry of the screen, not merely "clean, modern, spacious."
- **Color roles:** which family guides action, which surfaces support content, and when can an
  accent appear? Image colors also consume attention. Unused secondary tokens need not appear.
- **Typography:** choose the reading face first. Match language, weight availability, numerals,
  density and tone. Justify a second face only through a specific role.
- **Imagery:** decide crop, scale, lighting/background consistency and role before composing. For
  commerce, product identity and material matter. Do not fake approved assets or benefits.
- **Restraint:** name what stays quiet. One expressive feature needs ordinary neighbors.

Do not make "safe vs risky" the whole rationale. A risky font does not compensate for a weak layout.
Compare hypotheses for the same product, not three unrelated demographic personas.

## Brief synthesis: facts vs inferences

Before proposing directions, separate what the user actually said (verified facts, constraints,
fixed brand colors/fonts) from what you are inferring (explicit defaults). Do not infer a
demographic stereotype: "young" does not mean childlike type or three saturated accents; "mature"
does not mean every app becomes neutral, serif or minimal.

## Proposing 2–3 directions

- All directions answer the SAME brief: same audience, task, content and hard constraints. Keep
  fixed brand colors, required type and platform rules in every candidate.
- Each pair of directions differs in **at least two visual dimensions** — palette treatment,
  typography scale/weight, spacing/density, image treatment, geometry, icon character, depth. A
  different business flow or rearranged copy is NOT a different art direction.
- Name each direction by its visual identity (e.g. "Quiet grid, ink accent"), and state its
  proposition, its visible differences from the others, and its main tradeoff.
- A shared font, brand hue or radius across candidates is often correct; do not change things just
  to manufacture variety.
- If the user has fixed the entire system, offer one faithful direction instead of fake variety.

## Example: affordable discovery shopping for adults

"Young + affordable" does not imply neon orange, cartoon type and pill-shaped everything. Keep
identical products, labels and actions across candidates. Possible visual hypotheses are an airy
typographic composition with restrained color, a geometric system with bold flat color blocks, and
an image-led system with fine framing and quiet text. Choose only hypotheses supported by the
actual brief; these examples are not mandatory presets. Never invent discount evidence, ratings,
stock urgency or delivery promises to make the composition more attractive.

## Iterating with the user

- The user's feedback determines what becomes invariant. Preserve the decisions they approved.
- Adjust one visual cause at a time. Fix the biggest problem first (e.g. three equal focal points →
  remove two highlights) instead of piling another decorative treatment onto the same layout.
- If repeated tweaks don't improve hierarchy, change the composition hypothesis instead of
  rotating hues.
- On "switch theme", form new hypotheses informed by the feedback — not a random new palette.

## Extract after approval

Derive spacing, type hierarchy, color roles, shape and states from the approved composition. The
catalogue's breadth is a coverage tool, not the product design itself. Components are supporting
actors: do not force the signature treatment onto every component.
